Ceremony Playlist Builder

Turn your Spotify playlists into structured playlists for ceremonies, sessions, and other long events.

What is this?

What it does. Ceremony Playlist Builder makes long, ordered playlists from music already in your Spotify account. Describe the shape of an event, for example 30 minutes from one playlist, then 60 minutes from another, then 30 from a third. The app builds as many complete playlists as your music allows, with songs picked at random and no song repeated across the playlists made from one format. Each playlist is created in your Spotify account, ready to play.

What it's good for. Ceremonies, sessions, workshops, classes, and any long event where the music should follow an arc, such as arriving, deepening, and landing. Instead of steering a shuffle during the event, set the structure once and get fresh, ordered playlists as many times as needed.

How it works.

  1. Connect Spotify through a free developer app. This takes a few minutes, once.
  2. Write a format: which of your playlists to draw from, in what order, and for how long.
  3. Preview the plan, then create the playlists.

What you need. A Spotify Premium account (Spotify requires it for the free developer app) and some playlists of your own to draw from.

Good to know. Your playlists are only read, never changed. New playlists are private. Everything runs in this browser, and nothing about your music goes anywhere except to Spotify.

2. Your formats

How to edit formats

A format is a recipe for one kind of playlist: which of your playlists to draw from, in what order, and for how long. The editor below starts with a template. Replace its placeholder names, such as "source playlist 1", with the names of your own Spotify playlists. The shaded boxes in this help are samples to read, not places to type.

The parts of a format

Here is an example of a complete format. It makes 80-minute playlists: 20 minutes from a playlist called "Warm up", then 40 minutes from "Deep focus", then 20 more from "Warm up".

Each part does something different:

PartWhat it doesExample
The keyThe format's short name. It appears in the dropdown under Preview. Use lowercase letters, numbers, and hyphens."warm-focus"
nameThe template for the playlists this format creates. {n} becomes 1, 2, 3, and so on."Warm focus {n}"
silence between segmentsThe default pause between segments."30 sec"
segmentsThe list of playlists and minutes, played in order.("Warm up", 20),

Name it

Change the key to something short, such as "warm-focus". Then change the name line to the pattern you want for your playlists. Keep {n}, which becomes 1, 2, 3, and so on as the app creates each playlist. You can also use {letter}, which becomes a, b, c:

That makes playlists named "Warm focus 1", "Warm focus 2", and so on. Pick a pattern that none of your other playlists match. When you create playlists again from this format, the app treats playlists that match the pattern as its own and offers to replace them.

Point the segments at your playlists

Each line in segments is one stretch of music: a playlist name and a number of minutes. Replace the names and minutes with your own. Copy each playlist name from Spotify and paste it between the quotation marks. Capitalization and extra spaces at the ends don't matter, but spelling, punctuation, and emoji do. Only playlists you own can be used.

Add a segment by adding a line, and remove one by deleting its line. In the sample above, the three segments add up to 80 minutes of music per playlist.

Each segment typically lands within about 30 seconds of its target. Keep the punctuation exactly as shown: quotation marks around each name, a comma after the minutes, and a comma after each closing parenthesis. A playlist can appear in more than one segment, but no song repeats across the playlists made from one format.

Silence

The silence between segments line sets the default pause between each pair of segments. Set it to "none" for no pause, or to any length in steps of 30 seconds, such as "1 min" or "2.5 min". To use a different pause at one spot, add a silence line between two segments. It replaces the default there:

Pauses are extra time on top of your segment minutes. If you remove both the default line and any silence lines, the segments play back to back. Silences can be used for effect, or simply as a marker that tells a facilitator the music has moved into a new segment.

If something goes wrong

  • A red message under the editor means the formats couldn't be read. It's usually a missing comma, quotation mark, or bracket. Check the line it mentions and the one above it. Make sure the quotation marks are straight (") and not curly (“ ”), which can sneak in if you paste from a word processor.
  • A preview that says it could not find a playlist named something means that name doesn't match a playlist you own. Copy it again from Spotify, including any emoji.
  • Editing the formats clears any preview, so click Preview plan again afterward.

Why back up? Your formats are saved in this browser as you type, but clearing your browser data erases them. A backup file keeps a copy on your computer. Restore it if your formats ever disappear, or to use them on another computer. It contains your formats only, not your Spotify sign-in.
