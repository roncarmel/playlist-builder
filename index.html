Ceremony Playlist Builder

Turn your Spotify playlists into structured playlists for ceremonies, retreats, and other long events.

What is this app?

What it does
Ceremony Playlist Builder makes long playlists from your existing Spotify playlists. Describe the structure of the playlists you want to create, and the app builds as many complete playlists as your music allows, with songs picked at random and no song repeated across the playlists made from one format. Each playlist is created in your Spotify account, ready to play.

What it's good for
Ceremonies, retreats, workshops, and any long event where the music should follow an arc, such as arriving, deepening, and landing. Instead of steering a shuffle during the event or manually building a playlist ahead of time for each event, set the structure once and get multiple fresh, non-overlapping playlists. You can then use these playlists for subsequent events without ever repeating the same music.

How it works

  1. Connect Spotify through a free developer app. This takes a few minutes to set up, but you’ll only need to do it once.
  2. Define your format(s): which of your playlists to draw from, in what order, and for how long.
  3. Preview the plan, then create the playlists.

What you need
A Spotify Premium account (Spotify requires it for the free developer app) and some playlists of your own to draw from.

Good to know
Your existing Spotify playlists are only read, never changed. Playlists created by this app are private. Everything runs in this browser, and nothing about your music goes anywhere except to you and Spotify. If you run into a problem you can report it in the More options section at the bottom of this page.

2. Edit your formats

Your formats are saved in this browser as you type. You can any time.

How formats work

A format is a recipe for one kind of playlist: which of your playlists to draw from, in what order, and for how long. The app makes as many playlists from a format as your songs allow, and no song repeats between them.

Segments

Each row is one stretch of music. Tap the playlist to choose it, and use the minus and plus buttons to set how many minutes it fills. Drag the dotted handle to change the order. Each segment typically lands within about 30 seconds of its target. Point at a row, or tap a piece of the bar at the top, to see which belongs to which. Add another with New segment.

Silence

Choose Silence at the top of a row's playlist list to turn that row into quiet time. Silence can go between segments, or before or after the music, and it adds time on top of your segment minutes. It can be used for effect, or simply as a marker that tells a facilitator the music has moved into a new segment.

Mixes

To blend two playlists in one segment, tap the three dots on that row and choose Add a mix. Pick the second playlist, then use the slider to set how much of each. In the bar at the top, a mixed segment shows as diagonal stripes in two shades of pink.

Names

The name of a format is also the name of the playlists it makes: a format called "Warm focus" makes "Warm focus 1", "Warm focus 2", and so on. Give every format a different name. When you create playlists again, this app treats playlists that match a format's name as its own and offers to replace them.

If something goes wrong

  • A row that says "Not found in your playlists" names a playlist that was renamed or deleted. Tap it and choose the playlist again.
  • Spotify only lets this app read playlists you own, so those are the ones you can choose.
  • Changing a format clears any preview, so click Preview plan again afterward.

3. Preview and create

Preview shows the playlists this would make, with songs picked at random. If it looks right, a Create button appears below the plan, and it creates exactly what you see. Changing anything above clears the preview, so you preview again.
